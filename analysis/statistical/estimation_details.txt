Statistical estimation details

We report descriptive outcomes by condition and advice correctness, followed by exploratory mixed-effects models with crossed participant and item random intercepts. Analyses use Python 3.9.6, NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.13.1, and statsmodels 0.14.6. Binary models use \texttt{BinomialBayesMixedGLM}: fixed effects have independent $N(0,2^2)$ priors, and the log standard deviations of participant and item random intercepts have independent $N(0,1)$ priors. Mean-field variational Bayes uses a factorized Gaussian posterior approximation, maximizing the evidence lower bound with BFGS (gradient tolerance $10^{-5}$; maximum 2,000 iterations). Fits from seeds 42 and 123 converge, with fixed-effect posterior means differing by less than $2\times10^{-6}$.

The final-alignment model includes condition, AI correctness, their interaction, initial alignment, and standardized initial confidence. The advice-adoption model uses the same condition-by-correctness structure and initial confidence within initially non-aligned trials ($n=569$). We z-standardize confidence using the population SD across all 1,750 trials for correctness and alignment models; for adoption, scaling uses the 1,400 AI-present trials before selecting the risk set. Supporting correctness models compare all conditions with C0 and AI-present conditions with C1, adjusting for initial correctness and confidence; the AI-present model also includes condition-by-correctness interactions.

A Gaussian linear mixed model analyzes final chosen-answer Brier scores on AI-present trials, including condition, AI correctness, their interaction, and initial Brier score. We implement crossed random intercepts as participant and item variance components within one common group. Maximum-likelihood estimation uses L-BFGS; a Powell check converges to the same solution. The fit flags small random-effect variances (item 0.00048; participant 0.00097), so its Wald intervals warrant caution.

We prioritize adjacent-condition contrasts within each AI-correctness stratum. Binary-model intervals use the contrast mean $\pm1.96$ SD under the diagonal variational covariance, then exponentiate to odds ratios (ORs). Brier contrasts use the full fixed-effect covariance for 95\% Wald confidence intervals. We apply the Holm adjustment to approximate two-sided normal-tail values across three contrasts within each model and correctness stratum; none meets $p<.05$. Sensitivity analyses add displayed model score to the alignment model and exclude six sessions under eight minutes. Interface-stage duration is an interpretive process measure, not a primary endpoint.
